Virginia Commonwealth University hosted a full-scale, multijurisdictional emergency response exercise at the Stuart C. Siegel Center on Wednesday, Sept. 30, simulating a mass casualty incident on campus.

## Scope of the drill

The exercise ran from about 7 a.m. to 2 p.m. and involved more than a dozen state and regional agencies, local health systems and emergency managers, with over 250 responders, role players and exercise leaders. VCU emergency managers spent more than eight months planning it and describe it as the largest exercise of its kind the university has held.

Participants included VCU, VCU Health, HCA, Bon Secours, the Virginia Department of Emergency Management, Richmond police, fire, ambulance and emergency communications, Henrico and Chesterfield fire departments, and the Virginia Hospital and Healthcare Association. Several universities, including the University of Virginia, James Madison University and Liberty University, assisted.

## Purpose

The goal was to test coordination among first responders and the ability of area hospitals to receive patients under local, regional and state emergency plans.

## Community impact

Signs were posted ahead of time reading "VCU Police Exercise — Simulated Emergency." Sections of West Marshall Street and North Harrison Street were closed to make room for emergency vehicles starting at 6 a.m., and drivers were urged to use caution near the Siegel Center.

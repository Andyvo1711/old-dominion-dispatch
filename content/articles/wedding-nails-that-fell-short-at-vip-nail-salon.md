An appointment booked months in advance for a wedding carries far more weight than a routine visit, and the salon that accepts it takes on that importance too. This report on Vip Nail Salon describes a bride's experience where a few avoidable gaps, in communication, in technique, and in cleanliness, added up to a disappointment on nails she'd planned well ahead of her big day.

## A Front Desk That Didn't Communicate

The visit started coolly at reception, with a bit of an attitude and a curt "check in on the iPad," even after the client explained she had a 12:30 appointment. More importantly, the front desk never passed along the full booking. The client had also reserved a pedicure as part of an appointment made months earlier, but when she mentioned it after her nails were done, the technician looked confused, having never been told. As she put it, "where is the communication?" A booking is only useful if every service on it reaches the technician.

## One Coat, and Natural Nails Showing Through

The nail service itself, a Gel X almond French tip, started well, and the French tip shape looked good. The problem was the base: only one coat was applied, so her natural nails were visible underneath. A sheer, single-coat base undercuts the whole look, and on wedding nails, where every detail is meant to be flawless, that shortcut is especially disappointing. She noted she didn't even want to photograph her ring because of how the nails turned out.

## Cleanliness, and the One Bright Spot

To be fair, the client said the pedicure was the only good part of the visit. But the overall cleanliness of the space fell short, and she described it as filthy. For a total of $150, and for such an important occasion, the combination of a communication slip, a rushed base, and a less-than-clean environment left her feeling the visit simply wasn't worth it.

## Lessons for Salon Owners

- Pass the full booking to the technician. Every service on an appointment, especially a multi-part one, needs to reach the person doing the work. Confirm the whole appointment at check-in.

- Make reception warm, particularly for special occasions. A bride arriving for wedding nails should feel welcomed, not rushed through an iPad.

- Apply proper coats. Build the base so it's opaque and the natural nail doesn't show through, and give special-occasion sets an extra quality check before the client leaves.

- Keep the space clean. Cleanliness is foundational, and it's the first thing a client notices and remembers.

- Rise to the occasion. A long-planned wedding appointment is a chance to go above and beyond, not a routine slot. Treating it that way earns the kind of review, and referrals, that follow a bride for years.

For a client who planned this far ahead, the disappointment wasn't about impossible expectations, it was about ordinary steps missed on an extraordinary day. Clear communication, a properly built base, and a clean, welcoming space would have made these the wedding nails she was hoping for.

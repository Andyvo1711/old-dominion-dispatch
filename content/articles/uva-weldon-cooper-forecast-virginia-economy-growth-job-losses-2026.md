Virginia's economy is expected to return to modest growth in 2026, but a shrinking labor force and job losses point to continued weakness, according to a forecast from the University of Virginia's Weldon Cooper Center.

## Key projections

- GDP growth of 0.5% in 2026, versus 2.1% nationally
- About 18,050 jobs lost in 2026, the second straight annual decline; employment up 0.6% in 2027 and 1.1% in 2028
- Unemployment near 3.9% through late 2026
- Virginia inflation of 3.1% in 2026, up from an earlier 2.6% forecast
- Home values up 1.1%, rents up 3.4%, and home sales down 1.8%

The labor force has shrunk by roughly 68,000 people since early 2025 because of retirements and departures from the state.

## Where jobs are moving

Virginia lost about 9,100 jobs in the first half of 2026, with declines in professional services, manufacturing, administrative support, and accommodation and food services. Health care added about 2,800 jobs, its weakest pace in five years. Local government added about 9,400 jobs, while federal employment fell about 2,900.

Plumbing company president Derek Harper said he is short-staffed because "there's nobody to hire," while a staffing executive described a skills mismatch between job seekers and open roles. UVA economist Terry Raphann said Southwest Virginia is "getting new industries" and becoming more competitive.

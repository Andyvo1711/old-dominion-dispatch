A nail appointment is, at its core, a service built around a client's specific request, whether that is a certain shape, a particular color, or a design she has in mind. When that communication breaks down, the result can leave a client feeling unheard even if the technical work itself is not the main issue. That appears to be what happened at Pro Nails 3, where a client says her acrylic set arrived in the wrong shape and color, and began lifting within a week. This case looks at where the visit went off track and what could help prevent a repeat.

## A Request That Wasn't Followed

According to the client, she asked for a square shape but received an oval one, and the color applied did not match what she had requested. She also says the technician was unable to complete a specific design she had asked for. Shape and color are two of the most fundamental preferences a client communicates before a service begins, and getting either wrong is likely to be noticed immediately, unlike more subtle issues that might only surface days later. When a technician cannot execute a specific design, the better approach is to say so clearly before starting, so the client can decide whether to adjust her request or find another option, rather than discovering the mismatch only once the service is complete.

## Back and Forth That Didn't Resolve Anything Without Feeling Heard

The client describes repeatedly raising her concerns during the appointment, only to have her requests declined each time. She characterizes this exchange as frustrating and the customer service overall as falling short. Even when a technician has a good reason for suggesting a different shape or approach, such as the health of the client's natural nails, that reasoning needs to be explained rather than simply repeated as a refusal. A client who understands why a request cannot be fulfilled as asked is far more likely to leave satisfied, even if the end result differs slightly from what she originally pictured, than one who feels her input was dismissed outright.

## A Set That Didn't Hold Up

On top of the shape and color issues, the client reports that the acrylic began lifting within about a week, short of the typical two to three weeks a well applied acrylic set should last. Early lifting usually points to insufficient prep of the nail plate or inadequate curing, similar to issues seen in other early-peeling cases. Combined with a service that already left the client dissatisfied with the shape and color, a short-lived set compounds the sense that the visit did not deliver value for the price paid.

## Editorial Perspective

This case points to a few lessons for salons. First, shape and color are foundational preferences that should be confirmed with the client before work begins, since a mismatch here is immediately visible and hard to walk back. Second, when a technician cannot deliver a requested design, explaining the reason clearly builds far more trust than simply declining the request without context. Third, proper prep and application technique remain essential even when other parts of a service have already gone wrong, since a set that also fails to last only deepens a client's disappointment.

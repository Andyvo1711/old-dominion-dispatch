A client who says she wanted to keep coming back to ANS Nail Care & Spa describes her most recent visit as the one that finally changed her mind. Her account brings together three separate issues, pacing, technique, and finishing touches, that together left her feeling the $65 she paid did not match what she received.

## Feeling Rushed Through the Service

The client says the appointment felt rushed, as though the technician was moving quickly to get to other customers waiting. Pacing pressure is a real challenge in a busy salon, but when a client can feel that urgency during her own service, it tends to come across as her time and comfort being treated as secondary. Building in realistic scheduling between appointments, or communicating with a client if the salon is running behind, can help reduce the sense of being rushed even on a genuinely busy day.

## Filing That Reportedly Touched Bare Skin

The more serious part of her account is that she describes a file being taken to her bare skin during the service, which she says was painful enough to bring her to tears. Filing that makes contact with skin rather than staying on the nail plate is a technique issue that should not happen regardless of how busy a shift is, and it is worth treating as a priority correction with whoever performed her service. A brief pause to check in with a client the moment any discomfort registers, rather than continuing through it, is a basic step that helps prevent a moment like this from escalating.

## Leftover Polish and a Request for a 5-Star Review

She also mentions that excess polish around her nails was not cleaned up, which she describes as a simple courtesy that was skipped, and that she was asked to leave a 5-star review immediately after her service. Asking for a review in the moment, especially right after a service that did not go well, can put a client in an uncomfortable position and is worth reconsidering as a practice, since a genuine review is more valuable when a client has had time to reflect on the full experience rather than being asked on the spot.

## Editorial Perspective

A few lessons stand out from this account. First, a client should never feel that speed is being prioritized over her comfort during a service, and clear internal scheduling can help technicians avoid that pressure. Second, any filing or buffing that makes contact with skin is a technique issue that deserves direct, immediate correction and retraining, since client pain during a routine service points to something going wrong that should not be repeated. Third, small finishing details like cleaning up excess polish are easy to overlook when time is tight, but they are often what a client remembers most, and skipping them alongside a request for an immediate five-star review can make the whole visit feel transactional rather than genuinely accommodating.

A gift booking carries a little extra trust, because someone is planning a surprise and counting on the details to hold. This report on Aesthetics Day Spa & Nails describes a Mother's Day gift that did not go as booked, and while it reflects the customer's own account of the experience, every issue in it points to a standard any spa can protect: accurate pricing, delivering the services booked, and clear communication before any change.

## A Booking, a Reschedule, and Services That Changed

The customer booked online for his wife, choosing a Relax and Renew head-spa package listed at $99 and adding a $45 haircut, so the whole plan centered on a relaxing spa day. He received a text confirmation, then a call saying the date was not available and he needed to reschedule. He rebooked for a new time and received a second text confirming the correct services, the package plus the haircut. When his wife arrived, though, she was asked what she wanted rather than simply being given the services already booked, and by his account the spa package was never actually provided. When a client books specific services and confirms them twice, those are what should be ready on arrival, without starting the decision over at the chair.

## A $300 Bill Instead of the Price Booked

The bigger issue was the cost. The visit ended with a $300 bill for a haircut and a hair treatment, well beyond the roughly $144 of services that had been booked. The wife had agreed to add a hair treatment and was willing to cover the extra herself, specifically so her husband would not pay more than he expected, and she was reassured that he had planned for about $150, so she would not go over. Yet the haircut alone came to $150, when it had been offered online at $45, and the relaxing package she was supposed to receive never happened. Afterward, the customer was told the website prices were outdated and incorrect. A price a business advertises is a promise to honor, and if a listed rate is wrong, the fair response is to honor the booked price and correct the site, not to pass the difference to a customer who booked in good faith.

## Editorial Perspective

The encouraging part is that all of this is about systems and communication, which are fixable. First, keep the website accurate: prices and real appointment availability should match what a customer will actually receive, and any listed price should be honored even if it needs updating later. Second, deliver the services that were booked and confirmed, rather than restarting the choice when the client arrives, so a package a customer paid for is actually provided. Third, communicate any change and its cost clearly and get genuine agreement before proceeding, especially when the visit is a gift and the recipient is trying to respect a set budget. And when outside contractors are used, the spa should coordinate their pricing with what was booked, so the client is never caught between the two. Handled with accurate pricing and honest, up-front communication, this surprise gift would have been the relaxing day it was meant to be, and earned the family's trust rather than losing it.

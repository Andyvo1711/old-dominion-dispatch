A review of Diva Nails Spa from a family member shows how a single moment at the payment counter can shape how a whole family feels about a salon. She wrote on behalf of her grandmother, a returning client, and she describes a visit that ended with strong frustration. What stands out is that the family has been to the salon before, which means this feedback comes from people who were willing to return and who care about the experience.

## A Minimum Tip Mentioned at Checkout

The reviewer says her grandmother wanted only her toes done. When it was time to pay, she says her grandmother was told that a minimum tip of $10 applied, before she had written in a tip herself. The reviewer feels this was not explained beforehand, and she wonders whether the same rule is shared with every guest, since she noticed no sign about it.

Tipping is a sensitive topic for clients, since it is a personal gesture of thanks. When a salon has a minimum or suggested amount, the best approach is to make it visible and to explain it before the service starts. A small sign at the front desk, a note on the price menu, and a brief mention when the guest sits down all help. It is also worth letting the client choose the amount at the end, because a tip that feels voluntary is usually larger and carries more goodwill than one that feels required. Extra care is wise with older guests, who may feel more rushed or uncertain at the counter.

## Pricing for Children

The reviewer adds that this was not her family's first concern. She says that on an earlier visit, when her grandmother brought her daughters along, the salon charged an adult price instead of a child's price. At the time, she says, she let the matter go.

Many salons offer a separate menu for children, with a set age or size limit. The simplest solution is to print that policy and keep it at the front desk, so every family can see what applies before the service begins. When a guest is unsure which price applies, the technician can confirm it out loud before starting, which avoids any disagreement afterward.

## Editorial Perspective

This account leaves three practical lessons. First, explain any minimum or suggested gratuity before the service begins, and display it where guests can see it. Second, publish clear pricing for children, including the age or size range, and confirm it at the start of each visit. Third, remember that a family often decides together where to spend their money, and a calm explanation or a flexible gesture at the counter can keep several generations of clients coming back. The reviewer even noted that she lives a long way from the salon, a reminder that loyal clients may travel far, and that a respectful goodbye is worth the extra effort.

VCU Health Community Memorial Hospital (CMH) in South Hill is working with two community colleges to recruit and train laboratory technicians who want to live and work near the hospital.

## The partnership

CMH partners with Halifax Community College in North Carolina and J. Sargent Reynolds Community College in Virginia. Students in their final semester at Reynolds complete clinical rotations at CMH, which lets the hospital evaluate them much as it would in a job interview. Students who commute long distances can stay in hospital dormitories originally built for family medicine residents.

## Why it matters

The CDC estimates about 14 billion lab tests are performed in the U.S. each year, informing roughly 70% of medical decisions, yet hospitals nationwide struggle to recruit and retain lab professionals. CMH's lab handles clinical chemistry, hematology and molecular microbiology and supplies blood products and transfusion coordination for surgical and trauma patients.

"Laboratory science is often a hidden but highly rewarding career," said Chrystal Winstead, director of laboratory services. Recent Halifax graduate Caleb Richardson, now a full-time CMH lab technician, said the on-campus dorm let him rest and study rather than make a long daily commute from North Carolina.

A technician can start with a two-year associate degree; medical laboratory scientists usually need a bachelor's degree and handle more complex work.

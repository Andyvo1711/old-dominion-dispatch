VCU Health's LifeEvac air medical program is marking 25 years in 2026, a quarter century after it began flying patients from a single base at Richmond International Airport.

## Growth since 2001

LifeEvac extends the reach of VCU Medical Center, a Level I trauma center in downtown Richmond, flying or transporting patients from emergency scenes. It logged roughly 600 flights in its first year and now averages about 1,400 a year, with bases in Hanover County, West Point, Petersburg and South Hill. In 2021 VCU Health and LifeEvac formed an air medical alliance with Bon Secours Mercy Health, Riverside Health System and Mary Washington Healthcare.

## The crews

Each flight crew includes a pilot, a certified critical care flight nurse and a critical care paramedic, and each aircraft carries equipment typically found in an intensive care unit. Recruits need three to five years of prior paramedic or nursing experience, complete an eight-day Critical Care Academy and then about six months of orientation. Crews train quarterly and use Crew Resource Management techniques; before each shift, members complete a self-risk assessment covering factors such as sleep.

"Every call is a chance to make a difference for people," said flight nurse Beverly Harris, who has been with the program 21 years. Program medical director Harinder Dhindsa said safety is "really about getting the right people in the right seats."

The program also remembers flight paramedic Nikki Kielar and pilot Joe Schaefer, who died in a 2005 crash of a partner-operated aircraft.

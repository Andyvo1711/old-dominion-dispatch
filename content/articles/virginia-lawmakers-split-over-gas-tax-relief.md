As gasoline prices climb nationwide, Virginia lawmakers are divided over whether the Commonwealth should temporarily suspend or change its gas tax.

## What it would take

Any gas-tax holiday in Virginia would require action from the General Assembly. Because the legislature is not in session, Gov. Abigail Spanberger would first have to call a special session, and both chambers would need to approve a bill. Republicans pushed gas-tax relief in the most recent session, but it lacked enough Democratic support to advance. Indiana, Utah, Kentucky and Illinois currently have some form of gas-tax cut, freeze or suspension. WSET asked the governor's office whether she would consider a holiday and had not received a response when the story was published.

## The two positions

Del. Wendell Walker, a Republican, supports a temporary suspension and estimates it could save drivers about $5 per fill-up of a 20-gallon tank. He argues high fuel costs also raise grocery prices through transportation and delivery. "Any tax relief would be beneficial to the consumers," Walker said, and he asked why Virginia can offer a tax holiday for back-to-school supplies but not for fuel.

Del. Sam Rasoul, a Democrat, opposes a holiday, saying it could put transportation funding at risk for projects such as I-81 improvements, bridge repairs and unpaved roads. "We need to be taking care of our priorities here at home," he said, arguing Virginia should not bear the costs of international conflicts.

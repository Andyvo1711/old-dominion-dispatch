Virginia Commonwealth University unveiled a Virginia historical marker this month honoring the Gay Alliance of Students (GAS), the university's first LGBTQ+ student organization, capping a year of 50th-anniversary events.

## A two-year legal battle

In October 1974, VCU's Board of Visitors denied official status to the group. Students challenged the decision, and in October 1976 the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit ruled in *Gay Alliance of Students v. Matthews* that the refusal violated the First and Fourteenth Amendments. The ruling set a precedent for similar student groups in Virginia, Maryland, North Carolina, South Carolina and West Virginia.

## The marker

The Virginia Board of Historic Resources approved the marker in June 2026, and VCU sponsored it and covered the manufacturing cost. It stands on the lawn between University Student Commons and the T. Edward Temple Building. Walter Foery, who attended VCU from 1971 to 1976 and was a GAS member, cut the ribbon.

"When I had first joined GAS, I was incredulous that the university had denied recognition," Foery recalled. Archana Pathak, a faculty member in gender, sexuality and women's studies, said: "Never underestimate what a small, committed group of students can do."

Two King George Middle School students whose families sued their school board over a Gay-Straight Alliance also attended. That case cited the GAS ruling and ended in a September settlement allowing the group to form. VCU Libraries holds an archival collection on the GAS case covering 1974 to 1976.
